FICTIONAL TRAINING DOCUMENT — GLOSSARY AND RAG TEST QUESTIONS
Document ID: KB-001 | Version: 1.0 | Owner: Knowledge Management
This document includes definitions and test prompts for the fictional knowledge base.

GLOSSARY
Annual leave: Approved paid time away from work allocated under the employee's applicable agreement or policy.
Approval matrix: A document describing which roles can approve particular decisions or spend levels.
BM25: A keyword-retrieval method that ranks documents based partly on query-term frequency and document statistics.
Chunk: A segment of source text indexed and retrieved by a RAG application.
Embedding: A numerical representation of text used to compare semantic similarity.
ETL: Extract, transform, load; a process for moving and preparing data from source systems to target systems.
Faithfulness: The extent to which a generated answer is supported by the supplied evidence.
Hybrid search: A retrieval approach that combines semantic search with keyword-based retrieval.
Idempotency: A property where repeating an operation with the same logical input does not cause unintended additional effects.
LLM: Large language model, a model trained to process and generate text.
MFA: Multi-factor authentication, which requires more than one form of evidence to authenticate.
PII: Personally identifiable information; information that can identify or be linked to an individual.
Precision (retrieval): The proportion of retrieved passages that are relevant to the query.
Recall (retrieval): The proportion of relevant passages that the system successfully retrieves.
RAG: Retrieval-augmented generation, a pattern that supplies retrieved external information to a generative model.
Reranker: A model or algorithm that reorders candidate passages by relevance to a query.
Service-level target: A stated performance objective for a service, such as an acknowledgement time.
Watermark: A value used to track progress through incremental data processing.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS
Q: Is the ExampleWorks policy a real company's policy?
A: No. All policies and operational values in this project are fictional training examples.

Q: What is the annual leave entitlement in the sample leave document?
A: The fictional sample states 21 working days per completed leave year for eligible full-time employees.

Q: How early should planned annual leave be requested?
A: The sample says at least five working days in advance whenever practical.

Q: What is the sample payroll target?
A: The last business day of each month.

Q: Can someone work from another country without written approval?
A: No. The fictional remote-work document requires written approval from the manager and People Operations before cross-border work begins.

Q: Who is the CEO of ExampleWorks?
A: The sample documents do not specify a CEO's name. The assistant should say the information is not available in the indexed knowledge base.

Q: What is the exact expense approval limit?
A: The sample finance documents do not specify a universal monetary limit. The current finance approval matrix would need to be checked.

Q: Does the sample policy state a legal sick-leave entitlement?
A: No. It directs the employee to HR because actual requirements depend on location and employment terms.

Q: What should a user do if a policy answer is missing?
A: Check the retrieved source passages, search related documents, and ask the relevant document owner. The assistant should not invent a rule.

Q: Does local Ollama use mean data security no longer matters?
A: No. Local files, logs, model caches, and vector databases still need appropriate protection.

RAG TEST QUESTIONS — EXPECTED BEHAVIOUR
1. "How many annual leave days are available?" — retrieve the leave document and state the fictional entitlement with its eligibility condition.
2. "When do I need to request annual leave?" — retrieve the leave document and state five working days in advance whenever practical.
3. "What day is payroll?" — state the fictional target of the last business day of the month.
4. "Can I work remotely from another country?" — state that written manager and People Operations approval is required before work begins.
5. "What is the CEO's name?" — abstain; the dataset does not specify it.
6. "What is the expense approval cap?" — abstain on a numeric cap and direct the user to the current finance matrix.
7. "What should I do if I shared my password?" — report the possible exposure, change it through the official service, and contact Security.
8. "What is the difference between a risk and an issue?" — a risk is uncertain; an issue has already occurred.
9. "What does RAG stand for?" — retrieval-augmented generation.
10. "Can I paste confidential data into any AI tool?" — no; only use data permitted by the tool's approved classification and configuration.
11. "What should a pipeline record?" — run ID, start/end times, row counts, status, and useful error details.
12. "What is a good response to a missing policy answer?" — clearly state that the indexed documents do not contain enough evidence and suggest checking the document owner.
