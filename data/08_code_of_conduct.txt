FICTIONAL TRAINING DOCUMENT — CODE OF CONDUCT AND WORKPLACE RESPECT
Document ID: HR-003 | Version: 1.0 | Owner: People Operations
Fictional example for learning and testing; not legal advice.

1. Expected behaviour
Employees, managers, contractors, and visitors are expected to behave professionally and respectfully. Communication should be accurate, constructive, and appropriate to the workplace. Disagreement is acceptable when handled without threats, humiliation, harassment, or retaliation. Employees should consider the impact of their words and actions and help maintain an environment where people can raise concerns.

2. Equal opportunity and inclusion
Work decisions should be based on relevant skills, role requirements, performance, and legitimate business needs. Discrimination, harassment, bullying, and retaliation are not acceptable under this fictional standard. Managers should apply processes consistently and avoid assumptions based on personal characteristics. Accommodation requests should be handled confidentially and referred to HR where appropriate.

3. Harassment and bullying
Unwelcome conduct may occur in person, online, in messages, at work events, or during business travel. Examples include repeated insults, threats, unwanted sexual comments, targeted exclusion, humiliating jokes, or abuse of authority. A person who experiences or witnesses concerning behaviour may report it through the manager, HR, or the designated reporting channel. If the immediate manager is involved, the employee may use another channel. Reports should be handled impartially and with respect for confidentiality.

4. Retaliation
Retaliation against someone who raises a concern in good faith, participates in an investigation, or requests support is prohibited in this fictional policy. Retaliation may include threats, unfair exclusion, punitive scheduling, or adverse treatment linked to the report. Anyone who believes retaliation occurred should document relevant facts and contact HR or the reporting channel.

5. Conflicts of interest
A conflict may arise when personal, family, financial, or outside business interests could influence work decisions or appear to do so. Employees should disclose actual, potential, or perceived conflicts to their manager or Compliance before participating in the affected decision. Disclosure does not automatically mean wrongdoing; the purpose is to assess and manage the risk. Examples include evaluating a relative's company, accepting a valuable gift from a supplier, or making a purchasing decision involving a personal investment.

6. Gifts and hospitality
Gifts or hospitality must never be used to improperly influence a business decision. Employees should follow the current gifts register and approval process. Cash and cash-equivalent gifts should be declined. Where the value threshold is not stated in the available knowledge base, employees must check the current Compliance guidance rather than relying on an invented number. Gifts offered during a competitive procurement or contract negotiation require particular caution.

7. Confidentiality and public statements
Employees should not disclose non-public company, customer, vendor, or personnel information without authorization. Only authorized spokespersons may make official statements on behalf of the organization. Social media activity should not imply that personal opinions represent the company. Confidential information should not be shared as an example in a public presentation, portfolio, or AI prompt unless it has been approved for release.

8. Use of company resources
Company systems and equipment should be used lawfully and responsibly. Employees must not access another person's account, bypass controls, falsify records, or use resources for harassment or unlawful activity. Limited personal use may be permitted under a separate acceptable-use standard, but it must not interfere with work or create security risks.

9. Reporting and investigation
Reports should be made promptly and include relevant dates, people involved, witnesses, and supporting records where available. The reporter should distinguish firsthand observations from assumptions. Investigators should preserve relevant evidence, avoid unnecessary disclosure, and give involved people a fair opportunity to provide information. Confidentiality is maintained as far as practical but cannot be promised absolutely if a fair investigation or legal obligation requires disclosure.

10. Corrective action
A substantiated breach may lead to coaching, training, access restrictions, a formal warning, or other action consistent with the applicable process. The response should consider severity, intent, impact, prior relevant conduct, and mitigating circumstances. This fictional document does not prescribe a universal disciplinary outcome for every event.

Quick retrieval facts:
- Conflicts should be disclosed before participating in the affected decision.
- Cash gifts should be declined.
- Retaliation for good-faith reporting is prohibited in the sample policy.
- Employees should use another reporting channel if their manager is involved.
